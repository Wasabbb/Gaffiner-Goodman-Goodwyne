04. FOIA Requests

FOIA allows citizens to request access to certain public government records.

Information That May Be Covered

  • Marriage and divorces
  • Arrests, incidents and reports from law enforcement
  • Footage from government-owned properties
  • Past expungements
  • Commercial property registration information, including businesses and business properties
  • Additional information kept on record by a government agency

Common Exemptions

  • Classified information involving national security
  • Internal personal rules and practices
  • Information barred from release by another law
  • Trade secrets or confidential commercial/financial data
  • Privileged communications
  • Personal privacy information
  • Certain law-enforcement records
  • Phone numbers
  • Privately owned real estate
  • LEO disposition/punishments

What a FOIA Request Needs

  • Information sought
  • Department providing it
  • Names/call signs of officers who may have it, if applicable
  • Date ranges for events/incidents plus time

Submit via: Appointments → FOIA Inquiry

FOIA Timing

Confirmation or a request for clarification should be received within 5 days of submission. Most FOIAs should take no longer than 5 days. Complex requests may take up to 10 days.

Times are subject to change due to external agency communications or availability.

05. Expungement

Eligibility

Eligibility is determined by the type of offense, time since conviction or arrest, criminal history, and judicial approval. Not all offenses qualify.

Potentially Expungable

  • Certain dismissed charges
  • Minor offenses
  • Older convictions
  • Non-violent charges

What Expungement Means

Expungement does not always completely erase records. Some records may remain accessible to courts or law enforcement depending on state law.

Timing

Timing can depend on court backlog, record complexity, and judicial review timelines.

06. Civil Litigation

Can a Civil Case Settle?

Yes. Civil cases can settle outside court through negotiation, settlement agreements, or mediation, which may reduce court time and legal expenses.

How Long Does It Take?

Timing depends on court scheduling, complexity, evidence availability, and settlement negotiations. Some cases resolve quickly while others involve extended litigation.

Useful Evidence

  • Contracts
  • Screenshots
  • Financial records
  • Messages
  • Video clips
  • Witness testimony
  • Business agreements

Types of Civil Matters

  • Contract disputes
  • Property disputes
  • Financial disagreements
  • Business conflicts
  • Negligence claims
  • Breach of agreement cases
  • Certain lawsuits against individuals or organizations

07. Criminal Defense

Possible Outcomes
Charges may potentially be reduced or dismissed. Outcomes depend on available evidence, officer conduct, legal procedure compliance, witness credibility, prosecutorial decisions, and judicial rulings. No outcome is guaranteed.
Evidence Attorneys May Review
Bodycam footage, dashcam footage, screenshots, witness statements, arrest reports, video recordings, audio recordings, and MDT Records. These can be acquired by attorneys through FOIA.
Investigative Questions
The firm generally recommends waiting for legal counsel before answering investigative questions.
Active Police Scenes & Investigations
Attorneys may respond during active police scenes depending on availability; may attend interrogations, negotiate with law enforcement, and provide legal advice during investigations. Availability is not guaranteed during every emergency.
What Happens After Criminal Intake?
Intake staff reviews the request; an attorney may contact you for more information; evidence and reports are reviewed; representation terms are discussed; and the attorney prepares a legal defense strategy.

Criminal Matters

  • Arrests
  • Warrants
  • Felony charges
  • Misdemeanors
  • Drug charges
  • Weapons charges
  • Investigations
  • Traffic-related criminal offenses
  • Violations of state law

If Arrested

  • Remain calm
  • Do not resist arrest
  • Request an attorney immediately
  • Avoid unnecessary statements to law enforcement
  • Open a Criminal Intake ticket as soon as possible
